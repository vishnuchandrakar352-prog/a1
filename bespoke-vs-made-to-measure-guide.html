Bespoke vs Made-to-Measure

The words are often used loosely, but they describe very different ways of making clothes. Knowing the difference helps you choose and budget sensibly.

notes

  • Bespoke: new pattern, several fittings.
  • Made-to-measure: adjusted pattern, few fittings.
  • Ready-made: standard sizes, quick alterations.

i.

Off the rack

Ready-made clothing is cut to standard sizes for an average body. It is quick and affordable, and with simple alterations to sleeves and trousers it can fit many people well.

ii.

Made-to-measure

Made-to-measure starts from an existing pattern and adjusts it to your measurements. You choose cloth, lapels and pockets. The garment is usually made in a workshop or factory, and there may be one fitting or none.

iii.

Bespoke

Bespoke means a pattern is drafted from scratch for one person.

  • Dozens of measurements and notes on posture.
  • Two or three fittings on a loosely basted garment.
  • Much of the sewing done by hand, from the canvas to the buttonholes.

iv.

Which is right for you?

If your build is close to standard sizes, made-to-measure can be excellent. If you have sloping shoulders, a prominent seat or very particular taste, bespoke gives the tailor full control. Many people start with one made-to-measure suit and move to bespoke later.

v.

What to ask a tailor

Ask whether the pattern is drafted for you or adapted from a block, how many fittings are included, whether the jacket is fully canvassed and where the sewing is done. Good tailors are happy to explain.
