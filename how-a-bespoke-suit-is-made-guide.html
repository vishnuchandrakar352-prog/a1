How a Bespoke Suit Is Made

A bespoke suit is made over several weeks and several visits. Here is what happens at each stage, from the first tape measure to the final pressing.

notes

  • Typical time: six to ten weeks.
  • Fittings: usually two or three.
  • Your pattern is kept for next time.

i.

Consultation and measuring

The tailor asks how you will wear the suit, then takes thirty or more measurements and notes your posture: how your shoulders slope, how you stand and which arm is longer. You choose the cloth from heavy books of swatches.

ii.

Drafting the pattern

A cutter draws your pattern by hand on brown paper using chalk, square and curve. The pattern is kept, so future garments can be made from it and refined over time.

iii.

The basted fitting

The first fitting is a garment held together with loose white tacking.

  • The tailor checks balance, shoulders and length.
  • Changes are marked in chalk on the cloth.
  • The garment is taken apart and recut where needed.

iv.

The forward fitting

At the second fitting the jacket has its lapels, pockets and often its sleeves. Smaller changes to sleeve pitch, collar and waist are made, and the trousers are checked for length and break.

v.

Finishing

Buttonholes are sewn by hand, the lining is felled, and the whole garment is pressed into shape. A final fitting makes sure everything sits right. With care, a bespoke suit can last for many years and be altered as you change.
